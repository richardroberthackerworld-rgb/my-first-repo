Free AI Tools Every Indian Student Should Be Using in 2026

June 30, 2026 · 9 min read · by Shirandasu Sandeep

A short, honest list — tools that genuinely help you learn, not just finish homework faster. There is an enormous amount of noise around AI and studying right now, most of it trying to sell you something, so this is deliberately brief and blunt: the few categories that actually move the needle, and the rules that stop them from backfiring.

Here is the uncomfortable truth the marketing skips: most "AI study tools" help you finish homework, not learn. Those are opposite goals. Finishing faster feels great and leaves you exactly as ignorant as before; learning is slower in the moment and pays off in the exam hall. The tools below are chosen because, used correctly, they serve the second goal. The phrase "used correctly" is doing a lot of work, so we will get to the rules — but first, the short list.

For doubts: an AI tutor in your pocket

The first thing every student needs is somewhere to turn when they get stuck, because being stuck with nobody to ask is where self-study quietly dies. 7Solve answers any academic doubt — typed or photographed, handwritten or printed — and, crucially, does not just hand back an answer. You get the final answer, the concepts it depends on, the full working, and the common mistakes students make on that exact question, in English, हिंदी or తెలుగు.

Thirty free credits daily works out to three fully-explained doubts a day, every day, at zero cost — enough to cover a normal student's real sticking points. But the feature that deserves special mention is hint mode, because it reveals the right philosophy. It nudges you toward the method without giving away the answer, which is exactly how homework help should work: it gets you unstuck while leaving the actual thinking to you. A tool that only ever hands over finished answers makes you dependent; one with a good hint mode makes you capable. Reach for the hint first.

For practice: unlimited fresh papers

The second thing every student needs is a way to test themselves, because self-testing is the single highest-return study habit that learning science has ever measured — better, hour for hour, than any amount of re-reading. Its only real obstacle has always been supply: you run out of fresh questions, and re-doing ones you have memorised proves nothing.

7Marks removes that obstacle entirely. Give it any topic — or a photo of your own textbook pages — and it produces a complete question paper with an answer key: multiple-choice, fill-in-the-blanks, short and long answers, printable as a PDF. Because each paper is generated fresh, you never run dry, so you can test the same chapter repeatedly without simply recalling last time's questions. A doubt solver and a paper generator together cover the two halves of studying alone: getting unstuck, and proving to yourself that you are no longer stuck.

For writing: draft first, then improve

AI writing help is genuinely useful, but only in one direction, and the order matters more than anything else about it. Used as a second step it is excellent: write your paragraph or essay yourself first, then ask the AI to correct it and, importantly, to explain the corrections. Done this way, you see your own mistakes named and fixed, and your English steadily improves.

Used as a first step — asking it to write the thing for you — it does the exact opposite. It quietly replaces the skill you were supposed to be building, and every assignment makes you a little more dependent and a little less able. The tool is identical; only the order changes, and the order is the whole difference between growth and atrophy. Write first, always.

The ground rules

Notice the pattern running through all three: the same tool can help or harm depending entirely on how you hold it. That is why the rules matter more than the list. Three of them cover almost everything.

  • Understand, then copy — never the reverse. If a line is going onto your answer sheet, you should be able to defend every part of it under questioning. Copying something you cannot explain is borrowing a mark you will have to repay, with interest, in the exam.
  • Verify the numbers. AI can slip in long arithmetic, which is exactly why good tools show their working step by step — so you can check it rather than trust it blindly. Read the steps, especially on numerical problems, and treat the final value as a claim to be confirmed, not a verdict.
  • Fewer tools, deeper habits. There is a strong temptation to collect an app for every subject, but it mostly buys decision fatigue. One good doubt solver, one good paper generator, and your actual textbook cover about 95% of what any school, intermediate or degree student needs. Master a few tools deeply instead of sampling many shallowly.

In the end, the students genuinely winning with AI are not the ones with the most apps on their phones. They are the ones with the best loop — learn the topic, test yourself on it, ask about what you got wrong, and repeat a couple of days later. The tools on this list exist to power that loop. The loop itself is the thing that produces the marks.
