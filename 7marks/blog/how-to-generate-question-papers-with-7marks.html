How to Generate Question Papers with 7Marks (Full Guide)

July 6, 2026 · 9 min read · by Shirandasu Sandeep

From "I have an exam on Chapter 4" to a printable question paper with an answer key — in under a minute. This is the full guide to getting the most out of 7Marks, whether you are a student testing yourself, a teacher making worksheets, or a parent setting a Sunday revision quiz.

Every student knows that self-testing works better than re-reading. The problem has never been knowing it — it has been finding enough questions to test yourself with. Guidebooks run out, last year's papers get memorised, and making your own questions is slow and oddly hard to do well. 7Marks exists to remove that single obstacle, turning any topic into a fresh, complete paper on demand. Here is how to use it properly.

What 7Marks does

7Marks generates complete question papers and question banks on any topic you give it. That means the full range of question types you would see in a real exam — multiple-choice, fill-in-the-blanks, true or false, short-answer and long-answer questions — and, importantly, a full answer key alongside. It is not a quiz that leaves you guessing whether you were right; it marks itself.

The coverage is deliberately broad because Indian students study across a huge span of levels. It handles Class 1 through 10, Intermediate across all four streams (MPC, BiPC, CEC and MEC), every major degree course, and the professional exams — CA, CMA and US CMA — as well as competitive exams. And it works in English, हिंदी or తెలుగు, so a student revising in their mother tongue gets questions and answers in that language rather than an awkward translation. Whatever you are preparing for, the odds are it is covered.

Making your first paper

The first paper takes less time to make than reading this section. The steps are straightforward, but a couple of them reward a little thought.

  1. Open 7marks.7by.in and sign in with Google or a Gmail address. Every account gets 30 free credits daily, which is enough for real revision without paying.
  2. Choose your level, course and subject. As with any tuned tool, this is what calibrates the difficulty and style — a Class 8 science paper and a degree-level one should not look alike, and this is where you tell it which you want.
  3. Type the topic, and here specificity pays off. "Photosynthesis" works, but "Partnership Accounts — admission of a partner" works better, because a precise topic produces a precise, focused paper instead of a scattergun one. Alternatively — and this is the feature people love most — snap photos of your own textbook pages, and the questions will be drawn only from that exact material. That is perfect when a teacher has said "the test is from these three pages," because you can practise on questions built from precisely those pages.
  4. Pick your question types, the difficulty, and how long you want the paper, then tap Generate question bank. Within seconds you have a complete paper in front of you.

What you get

The output is built to be used, not just looked at.

  • A properly formatted question paper with marks allocated per question, so it reads and feels like a real exam paper rather than a loose list of questions.
  • A complete answer key. For multiple-choice questions you get the correct option; for numericals and problems you get the worked answer, so you can see not just what the answer was but how it was reached.
  • An export to PDF option for clean printing, and a shelf that quietly remembers your last twenty papers on the device — so a worksheet you made last week is still there when you want to reprint it or set it again.

How different people use it

The same tool solves quite different problems depending on who is holding it, and it is worth seeing yourself in one of these.

Students use it to self-test before exams, which is the single highest-return study habit that learning science has ever identified. The trick is to make a paper on a chapter after studying it, sit it closed-book, mark yourself honestly against the key, and treat every wrong answer as a precise instruction about what to revise next. Because each generated paper is fresh, you can do this repeatedly on the same chapter without simply memorising last time's questions.

Teachers and tuition centres use it to produce fresh, non-repeating worksheets without the hours of drudgery that making them by hand demands. When every batch gets a slightly different paper, copying becomes pointless and practice becomes real. It is also a quick way to differentiate — an easier set for one group, a harder set for another, generated in the time it used to take to photocopy.

Parents use it to set weekly revision tests, even for subjects they themselves do not remember, because the answer key does the marking for them. A ten-minute Sunday quiz on the week's chapters, marked together, keeps a child's learning honest in a way that "did you revise?" never can.

Free use and when you need Pro

Basic papers run comfortably on the free daily credits, so ordinary school and intermediate revision costs nothing at all. The Pro plan, at ₹47 a month, exists for the demanding end: hard and practical papers, and CA or CMA level material, where the questions and worked solutions are generated by the most capable AI models. If you are a professional-exam candidate generating serious practice sets daily, Pro is the tier built for you; for everyone else, the free 30 credits a day will carry your revision a long way. As always, you see what a paper needs before you spend anything on it.
