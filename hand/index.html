How it works

No template sheet to print, no scanner, no app to install.

STEP 01

Photograph a page

Old notes, a past assignment, anything in your own hand. Dark pen, flat on a table, daylight. A slight tilt is corrected automatically.

STEP 02

Confirm what it says

7Hand finds every line, word and letter, then matches them to the text. It only keeps the words it is certain about — so it never mislabels a letter.

STEP 03

Write anything

Type or paste your text. Set the writing size and line spacing to match your notebook. Download a PDF and print it at 100%.

Built to not look fake

The reason machine-written pages get spotted is that every letter is identical. 7Hand is built around avoiding exactly that.

Every letter is a real one of yours

It keeps several genuine samples of each character from your page and varies which one it uses. The same letter twice in a row never gets the same shape.

The writing drifts, like a hand does

Slant, size and baseline wander together and slowly — the way writing changes as a hand tires or speeds up. Not random jitter per letter, which reads as fake in its own way.

Sits on real ruling

Line spacing is set in millimetres and defaults to 8.5 mm, matching standard Indian ruled paper. Print at 100% and it lands on the lines.

Nothing leaves your device

Your photo, your letters and your text are processed entirely in your browser. There is no account and no upload. Optional AI reading is the one exception, and it asks first.

What it can’t do

Worth knowing before you spend twenty minutes on it.

Whatever your photo doesn’t contain, your style won’t have

Photograph a page of all lowercase and you get a lowercase-only handwriting. Capitals, digits and punctuation have to appear somewhere. The fix is one extra line, photographed as a second page.

Around half your words get skipped

Where letters touch each other, 7Hand cannot tell where one ends and the next begins — so it discards that word instead of guessing. Print-style writing keeps far more than joined-up cursive does.

It writes prose, not diagrams

Text on lines. Not circuit diagrams, ruled observation tables, graphs or formulae. A lab record page is often half diagram, and that half is still yours to draw.

English only, for now

Devanagari and other conjunct-forming scripts need different handling and are not supported yet.

The only way to know whether it looks like your handwriting is to try it with your handwriting.
