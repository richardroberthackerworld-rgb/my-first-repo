How to Make Instagram Reels That Actually Get Views

By VidLab·9 min read·Updated 2026

Reels are the single biggest reach engine on Instagram — a well-made one can put you in front of a hundred times your follower count, because unlike a feed post, a Reel is served to strangers who don't follow you yet. That's the opportunity. The problem is that the same system is ruthless: the average viewer decides whether to keep watching or flick to the next Reel in about two seconds, and the algorithm watches how many people stay. Making Reels that get views isn't about luck or a secret hack. It's about a handful of concrete craft decisions, and every one of them is free to apply.

This guide covers the whole workflow: the hook that wins the first two seconds, the correct size and length, captions and pacing, using trending audio, and how to edit a polished Reel free in your browser with no watermark stamped across it.

The first two seconds decide everything

Nothing else about your Reel matters if people leave before it starts. The opening two seconds — the hook — are the most important part of the entire video, and they should be treated that way. A strong hook does one of a few things: it shows the payoff up front ("here's the result"), it poses an irresistible question, it creates a small tension the viewer needs resolved, or it opens on genuine visual motion that stops the thumb. What a hook must never do is warm up slowly — no "hey guys," no logo, no setup. Start on the most interesting moment you have.

The test: watch your first two seconds with the sound off and ask, "would this make a stranger stop scrolling?" If the honest answer is no, re-cut the opening before you worry about anything else. The hook is 80% of the result.

Size, length and the safe zone

Get the technical frame right so the platform shows your Reel at its best. Reels are vertical, 9:16 — film and edit full-screen tall, ideally 1080×1920. A landscape video posted as a Reel gets shrunk into a tiny strip and looks out of place immediately.

On length, shorter usually wins for reach because it's easier to earn a high completion rate — many of the best-performing Reels are 7 to 15 seconds. Longer Reels can work when the content genuinely holds attention, but every extra second is another chance for someone to leave. And mind the safe zone: keep your text and key action out of the bottom quarter and the right edge, where Instagram stacks the caption, buttons and audio label over your video. Content placed there gets buried under the interface.

Pacing: why Reels feel fast

Watch any high-performing Reel and you'll notice it almost never holds a single shot for long. There's constant movement — cuts, zooms, text appearing, the subject changing — because every change re-captures the wandering eye and resets the "should I leave?" timer. Dead air, long static shots, and pauses are where viewers drop off. The fix is aggressive cutting: remove every gap, tighten every sentence, and keep something changing on screen at all times. This is the "jump cut" energy that defines the format, and it's created entirely in the edit by cutting out the slow parts.

A Reel that feels fast isn't filmed fast — it's cut fast. Remove every pause and dead moment and ordinary footage suddenly has momentum.

Captions are not optional

A large share of Reels play muted, at least for the first moment, so captions carry your message when the sound doesn't. On-screen text also boosts retention by giving restless eyes something to follow, and it makes your content accessible and searchable. The modern style is punchy, styled captions — often appearing word by word or line by line in sync with the speech, in a bold font with a clear outline so they read over any footage. Whether you go word-by-word or simple line captions, the non-negotiable is: put your key message in text, on screen, from the start. (For the full method, see our guide to captions.)

Trending audio and why it matters

Instagram's system pays attention to audio. Using a track that's currently trending can give a Reel an extra push, because the platform surfaces content tied to sounds people are engaging with. That said, trending audio is a boost, not a foundation — a great video with an ordinary sound beats a weak video riding a trend. Use trending audio when it genuinely fits your content, keep the audio you actually need (your voice, key sound effects) clear in the mix, and don't force a trend onto content it doesn't suit. And remember the copyright basics: stick to Instagram's in-app music and cleared audio libraries rather than pasting in a commercial track that could get your Reel muted.
